# Digital Dependence, Nomophobia and Sleep Hygiene Among University Students

## About the Project

This project investigates the relationship between smartphone use, nomophobia, anxiety, and sleep quality among university students.

The study uses primary data collected through a structured online questionnaire and applies statistical methods in R to examine the relationships between digital dependence and sleep-related outcomes, with anxiety investigated as a potential mediating factor.

## Research Objectives

The study aims to:

- Examine the relationship between smartphone use and sleep quality.
- Investigate the association between nomophobia and sleep quality.
- Examine the relationship between anxiety and sleep quality.
- Assess whether anxiety plays a mediating role in the relationship between digital dependence and sleep quality.
- Investigate whether these relationships differ by gender.

## Study Design

- **Study design:** Descriptive-analytical, cross-sectional study
- **Data collection:** Primary data through an online questionnaire
- **Sample size:** 274 university students
- **Sampling method:** Snowball sampling
- **Study population:** University students in Delhi
- **Statistical software:** R

The questionnaire included sections covering demographic characteristics, smartphone use patterns, nomophobia indicators, subjective sleep quality, and anxiety symptoms. Adapted items from established validated instruments were used.

## Statistical Analysis

The analysis was conducted in R and included:

- A priori power analysis
- Composite score construction
- Descriptive statistics
- Reliability analysis using Cronbach's alpha
- Shapiro-Wilk normality testing
- Spearman correlation analysis
- Mann-Whitney U tests
- Multiple linear regression
- Regression diagnostics
- Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) assessment
- AIC-based model comparison
- Mediation analysis
- Data visualisation

The complete R analysis script is included in this repository.

## Key Findings

The analysis found significant relationships between phone use, nomophobia, anxiety, and sleep quality.

Nomophobia and phone use were both associated with poorer sleep quality, while anxiety showed the strongest relationship with sleep quality among the variables examined.

Regression and mediation analyses further investigated the role of anxiety in the relationship between digital dependence and sleep quality.

No statistically significant gender differences were identified in the study.

The underlying primary dataset is **not included in this repository** in order to protect the anonymity and confidentiality of the study respondents.

## Limitations

The study has several limitations, including its cross-sectional design, snowball sampling approach, use of adapted shortened scales, and limitations associated with the mediation methodology.

Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as statistical associations rather than definitive causal relationships.

## Future Directions

Future research could consider:

- Bootstrapped mediation analysis
- Longitudinal study designs
- Broader and more diverse samples
- Full validated measurement instruments
- Additional factors such as caffeine, exercise, academic stress, and social support
- Replication across different Indian university populations
